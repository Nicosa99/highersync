CORE TEACHING

There are two orders to reality.

David Bohm called them the Implicate and the Explicate. The Explicate Order is the world you navigate every day: objects in space, events in time, a body moving through a life that seems to be happening to it. It has texture and temperature and consequence. It is entirely real. But it is not primary.

Beneath it, not in a spiritual sense, but in a precise physical sense, derived from the mathematical requirements of quantum theory, there is the Implicate Order. An undivided wholeness. A dynamic background field from which all observable matter continuously unfolds. Bohm called its organizing force “active information.” Not information as data, not information as pattern, but information as causal agent: a meaning-bearing signal that gives form to the movement of energy the way radar guides a ship. Not by pushing it. By informing it. By carrying the meaning that directs its course.

The critical word is meaning. In Bohm’s framework, only information that carries meaning for a given system becomes active and actually influences what happens. Information without meaning, noise, static, scattered attention, has no organizing force. It leaves the field neutral. But information charged with meaning, with specificity, with the full weight of directed inner state? That information enters the Implicate Order as an active organizing principle. And the Explicate unfolds around it.

This is not Bohm theorizing loosely. This is the formal ontological framework he developed to resolve the contradictions that standard quantum mechanics could not address. Where Copenhagen interpretation stopped at the wavefunction and called observation a given, Bohm traced the logic further and showed: reality has structure at two levels, and the organizing force operating at the deeper level is something that functions like mind.

The Hermetic tradition arrived at exactly this position. Two thousand years before Bohm. Without instruments.

The Principle of Mentalism, as encoded in the Kybalion and before that in the Corpus Hermeticum, states: The All is Mind; the Universe is Mental. This is not the claim that the physical world is imaginary. It is a structural claim: at the root of the universe is something that operates on meaning. Something that, when it has meaning, becomes active and begins to organize matter from the inside. Not from outside through force, but from inside through information.

Bohm’s active information is that something.

The gap between these two framings is linguistic. The territory they describe is the same.

If the Explicate Order is downstream from the Implicate, if physical circumstances continuously unfold from the active information being seeded into the field, then attempting to change the Explicate by working only in the Explicate is working against the direction of causality. You are rearranging the surface of something that is being continuously reorganized from underneath. Your circumstances do not primarily generate your inner state. With the same fidelity. In both directions.
